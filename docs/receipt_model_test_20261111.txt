Receipt reading: model test (2026-11-11)
=======================================

STATUS: PARTLY DONE. The OpenRouter key hit its own lifetime limit part-way through the first pass, so two of
the five models are not measured (see "What did not get measured"). Everything below is real output from real
calls; nothing is estimated except where it says so.

What was asked
--------------
Which vision model should read photographed receipts in the Receipt box: a cheaper one or a more accurate
one? Candidates: anthropic/claude-haiku-4.5, claude-haiku-5.5, claude-sonnet-5, claude-sonnet-5.5 and
google/gemini-3.5-flash-lite (all five exist in OpenRouter's model list).

Method
------
- The real reader code was used unchanged (apps/server/src/receipt-reader.ts): receiptPrompt (today
  2026-11-11, country United States), parseReceiptRead and reconcile. A model's "final" total is
  reconcile(read).total, so the numbers include what reconcile does with a doubtful or smudged total.
- 17 synthetic receipt pictures (scripts/receipt-model-test/make-receipts.mjs, drawn from HTML with Playwright's
  Chromium, 1x scale, no real receipts): clean grocery; retail with a 30-day return policy and "Visa ending";
  restaurant with a printed tip line plus a handwritten-style tip and total; faded low-contrast thermal print
  (cash); rotated 4 degrees and skewed; blurred; first digit of the total covered by a smudge (recoverable only
  from subtotal + tax); a long narrow receipt with 40 lines; top cut off with the store name only in a logo box;
  French receipt (04/11/2026, decimal commas, EUR); electronics with a 2-year protection plan; gas station in
  10 px text; two receipts side by side; a restaurant menu (not a receipt); pharmacy (Health, 28-day OTC
  returns); card printed as XXXX XXXX XXXX 7391; and the whole 16-digit card number printed (a made-up number).
  The answer key is scripts/receipt-model-test/truth.json. Every printed amount is added up in code.
- scripts/receipt-model-test/run.mjs sends each picture as a data-URI image_url plus the prompt text, temperature 0,
  max_tokens 1500, usage {include: true} (so OpenRouter reports the real cost of every call), three calls at a
  time per model, no retry on a bad answer (a bad answer is a failure; only 429/5xx would be retried, and none
  happened). Raw per-call JSON is kept outside the repo (session scratchpad), not here.
- Scoring per picture: merchant (case/accents/"&" ignored, either name containing the other), date exact,
  total within 0.01 (final = reconcile's total; the raw model total is recorded too), tax within 0.01 (missing
  and 0 count the same), last 4 exact, returnDays exact or null, category exact or one of the listed acceptable
  alternatives (café Dining/Groceries, electronics Other/Home, fuel Other/Travel, office supplies Other/Home),
  isReceipt, multipleReceipts. "key-4" = merchant + date + total + last 4 all right. "Danger" = the final total
  is wrong but reconcile said "sure" (the person would never be asked). Any answer containing a run of 12 or more
  digits (also with spaces or hyphens) would be a hard failure.
- Spend cap US$1.50: never close. Total spent was about US$0.235 (see the end).

Honest limits
-------------
- The pictures are RENDERED, not photographed. Real photos add glare, folds, shadows, perspective, thumbs,
  JPEG noise and low light, so real accuracy will be lower than these numbers and the models' order could change.
  Run scripts/receipt-model-test/README.txt on 20-30 of the owner's own receipts before trusting any figure here.
- 17 pictures and one run each: a difference of one or two receipts between models is noise, not a ranking.
- Temperature 0 does not make the models identical from run to run; no run was repeated (to save money).
- The handwriting is an italic font, not handwriting. The fake card number was chosen, not a real card.
- The score of each model is over the pictures it actually answered. Two models had a cut-off answer on one
  picture each (explained below); those count as failures in "parse" but their other fields are not counted.

To finish: raise or replace the key, then run (finished calls are kept, only the missing ones are paid for):
    OPENROUTER_API_KEY=... npx tsx scripts/receipt-model-test/run.mjs <images folder> --out <results folder>
The pictures can be made again with make-receipts.mjs (the same pictures, deterministic).

The failures worth reading
--------------------------
(model said / truth)
1. haiku-4.5, 01 clean grocery: date "2026-03-11" for a receipt printed 11/03/2026 (truth 2026-11-03). It read a
   US date in day/month order. 06 blurred: date "2026-03-11" for 11/09/2026 (truth 2026-11-09) and card last 4
   null (its own note said "ending 7762"; the truth is 7742). Nothing was marked unclear for the dates, so the
   person would have seen a wrong date without a highlight. Haiku-4.5 was the only model to misread a clean
   receipt.
2. haiku-4.5, 03 restaurant: total 90.23, tip 12, subtotal 72.50, tax 5.73 (truth by the prompt's own rule:
   printed total 78.23). It took the handwritten total. Because subtotal + tax + tip = 90.23, reconcile said
   "sure": counted as a dangerous error. The other three models reported 78.23 and wrote the handwritten 90.23 in
   "notes", as asked; reconcile then saw 72.50 + 5.73 + 12 (tip) = 90.23 against 78.23 and asked the person
   (candidates 78.23 and 90.23), which is a sensible outcome. The prompt contradicts itself here (see PROMPT AND
   RECONCILE FINDINGS 1).
3. haiku-4.5, 07 smudged: wrong silent guess of 63.40, caught by reconcile (above).
4. haiku-5.5, 04 faded thermal: merchant "SWEET FISE BAKERY" (truth Sweet Rise Bakery). It marked "merchant"
   unclear, so the Check card would highlight it. Not dangerous.
5. haiku-5.5, 10 French receipt: date "2026-04-11" (truth 2026-11-04; its own note says "read as 11 April 2026
   under US convention"). Not marked unclear; "2026-04-11" is a plausible date so plausibleDate() lets it
   through. See finding 3. Sonnet-5 got the same picture right (2026-11-04).
6. haiku-5.5, 08 long narrow (40 lines): answer cut off at the 1,500-token limit, in the middle of the item
   list (finish_reason "length"; 644 of the 1,500 tokens were hidden reasoning). Counted as a parse failure.
7. sonnet-5, 05 rotated/skewed: EMPTY answer. All 1,500 tokens went to hidden reasoning (reasoning_tokens 1500,
   content ""), so it said nothing. Counted as a parse failure.
   Both 6 and 7 are the token limit, not reading ability (finding 4). The app's retry-once would very likely
   hit the same wall, because the second try uses the same prompt and limit.

PROMPT AND RECONCILE FINDINGS (receipt-reader.ts was not changed)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
1. The prompt contradicts itself about tips. "total": "the final amount charged (including tax and tip)", but
   the Rules say: "If a handwritten tip ... changes the total, report the printed total and say so in notes."
   Evidence: haiku-4.5 followed the first and reported 90.23 with the tip 12 (reconcile: sure); the other three
   followed the second. Also the three that followed the rule still fill "tip": 12, which makes reconcile
   add 12 to the sums and then disagree with their own correct 78.23 (it asks the person; harmless but
   avoidable). Suggestion: say plainly "a handwritten tip is NOT part of the total or the tip field: leave
   tip null and describe it in notes", or decide that the written-in final total is what the person paid.
2. reconcile() cannot cope with tax-INCLUDED totals (VAT/GST/"dont TVA"/"taxes included"). Evidence: picture
   10. haiku-5.5 and sonnet-5 both answered total 18.80 (right), subtotal null, tax 1.42 (the included VAT) and
   six item lines adding up to 18.80. Line 195 of receipt-reader.ts then expects lines + tax = 20.22, finds the
   total contradicted, and returns status "pick" with candidates [18.8, 20.22]: a question about a correct
   total. Every receipt from a country (or any shop) that prints included tax will do this. (Haiku-4.5 avoided
   it only by inventing subtotal 17.38 = 18.80 - 1.42, i.e. doing the arithmetic the prompt forbids.)
   Suggestion: treat lines-sum (and subtotal) alone as acceptable expected values too, not only "+ tax + tip".
   The fuel receipt ("taxes included", no tax figure) is fine because there is no tax to add.
3. The date rule is tied to the person's country, not to the receipt. "The person lives in United States, so
   03/04/26 is March 4". A French receipt in euros dated 04/11/2026 was read by haiku-5.5 as 11 April (and not
   flagged), by sonnet-5 as 4 November. A US person who travels, shops online or keeps foreign receipts will
   hit this. Suggestion: add "if the receipt's currency, language or address is from another country, use
   that country's date order; if you cannot tell, return null and add 'date' to unclear". Also consider
   treating any date with day and month both 12 or less as unclear when the currency is not the person's.
4. max_tokens 1500 is too small for these models. Haiku 5.5, Sonnet 5 and Sonnet 5.5 think before they answer
   by default (reasoning tokens on 8 of 17, 10 of 17 and 3 of 4 calls; up to 644, 1,500 and 301 tokens) and
   the thinking is taken out of max_tokens. A 40-line receipt's JSON alone is about 900 tokens. Haiku-4.5 did not
   reason on any call (Gemini showed 0 reasoning tokens on its one trial call, which was discarded). The app should set a larger limit (4,000 or more) and ask for
   little or no reasoning if OpenRouter offers that for the chosen model (untested here: no credit was left).
   This matters twice: it caused both parse failures, and reasoning tokens are billed as output (they are
   already inside the costs above).
5. Good: no model leaked a card number, 0 of 55 answers contained 12 or more digits. All four models said
   "not a receipt" for the menu and "several receipts" for the two-receipt photo (the three models that answered
   those pictures: haiku-4.5, haiku-5.5, sonnet-5). Those three also read the fuel receipt's 10 px text and the
   cropped receipt's merchant from the logo ("little sprouts") correctly.
6. A silent wrong guess is the real risk and reconcile is what stops it: haiku-4.5's 63.40 on the smudged total
   was wrong with "unclear" empty, and only the receipt's own sums turned it into a question. Keep reconcile.

Recommendation (provisional: two of five models were not measured, and the pictures are not real photos)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Default reader: anthropic/claude-sonnet-5 at about 0.75 cents a receipt (about US$0.75 per 100). It got
  every picture it answered right (14 of 14 on all nine fields, including the smudged total, the French date
  and the handwritten tip handled by the rule) and had the best key-4 rate, 93.3%, the miss being the cut-off
  answer, not a misreading. Latency 1.9 s average, 3.1 s at p95.
  Sonnet-5.5 looked equally good on the four pictures it answered and costs the same (0.80 cents), so there is
  no reason yet to prefer it; decide after its other 13 pictures are done.
- Cheap option: anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5 at about 0.04 cents a receipt (US$0.04 per 100), 19 times cheaper
  than Sonnet, fastest (1.5 s average). It was right on everything it finished except one faded merchant name it
  marked unclear itself, and the foreign date (a prompt problem, finding 3). It is NOT free of risk: it
  also uses reasoning tokens and needs the larger max_tokens, and one 40-line receipt was cut off.
  A sensible design to try (not tested): read with haiku-5.5, and re-read with Sonnet only when the result is
  "pick", has anything unclear, or fails to parse. On this set that would be about 5 of 17 receipts, an average
  near 0.04 + 0.3 x 0.75 = about 0.27 cents a receipt, with Sonnet's accuracy on the doubtful ones.
- Do not use claude-haiku-4.5: seven times the price of haiku-5.5 and the only model that misread a clean
  receipt, misread dates twice, guessed a smudged total without saying so and took the handwritten total.
- Gemini 3.5 flash lite: no data. Run it after the key is fixed (list price US$0.30 per million input tokens;
  its one discarded trial call cost 0.12 cents, about twice haiku-5.5, so it is worth the test).
- Before choosing for good: (a) fix the prompt and reconcile points above and raise max_tokens, (b) run
  the README steps on 20-30 real receipts including the worst ones (crumpled, glare, thermal fading), (c) compare
  again; the real photos matter more than this table.
